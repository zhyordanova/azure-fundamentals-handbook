# Geo-distribution


## Definition

Geo-distribution is the ability to deploy applications and services across multiple geographic regions around the world.

Cloud providers such as Microsoft Azure operate datacenters in many regions, allowing organizations to place resources closer to their users.

## Why Geo-distribution Matters

Users expect applications to be available with fast response times regardless of their location.

Deploying applications across multiple Azure regions provides several benefits:

- Lower network latency
- Better user experience
- Improved business continuity
- Greater flexibility for global organizations

Geo-distribution allows organizations to deliver services from locations that are geographically closer to their customers.

## Azure Regions

Microsoft Azure operates datacenters in multiple regions worldwide.

Examples include:

- West Europe
- North Europe
- East US
- Central US
- Southeast Asia
- Australia East

Organizations can choose the region that best meets their business, performance, and compliance requirements.

## Common Mistakes

❌ Thinking Geo-distribution is the same as High Availability.

High Availability focuses on keeping applications running during failures.

Geo-distribution focuses on deploying applications in multiple geographic regions.

❌ Thinking Geo-distribution automatically provides disaster recovery.

Deploying resources in multiple regions supports disaster recovery, but additional architecture and replication strategies are required.

## Compare With

| Geo-distribution | High Availability |
|------------------|-------------------|
| Uses multiple geographic regions | Keeps services available during failures |
| Improves global performance | Improves uptime |
| Reduces latency | Reduces downtime |

## Exam Tip

- serve users around the world;
- place resources closer to users;
- reduce geographic latency;

> **Geo-distribution**

Geo-distribution primarily focuses on **geographic placement of workloads and users**.
