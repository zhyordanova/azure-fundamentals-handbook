# Capital Expenditure (CapEx) vs Operational Expenditure (OpEx)


## Definition

Organizations must decide how they acquire and pay for IT infrastructure.

The two primary financial models are:

- **Capital Expenditure (CapEx)** – investing in physical assets through upfront purchases.
- **Operational Expenditure (OpEx)** – paying for services as ongoing operational costs.

Understanding the difference between these models is fundamental to understanding the business value of cloud computing.

## Capital Expenditure (CapEx)

Capital Expenditure refers to one-time investments in physical infrastructure.

Examples include:

- Purchasing servers
- Buying storage systems
- Networking equipment
- Building or expanding a datacenter

These investments are typically made before resources are used.

### Characteristics

- Large upfront investment
- Organization owns the hardware
- Long procurement cycle
- Hardware maintenance is the organization's responsibility
- Typical for traditional on-premises environments

## Operational Expenditure (OpEx)

Operational Expenditure refers to ongoing expenses for services that are consumed over time.

Instead of purchasing hardware, organizations pay only for the services they use.

Examples include:

- Azure Virtual Machines
- Azure Storage
- Azure SQL Database
- Azure App Service

### Characteristics

- No upfront hardware investment
- Consumption-based pricing
- Monthly or usage-based billing
- Easier to scale costs with business needs
- Typical for cloud computing

## Compare With

| Capital Expenditure (CapEx) | Operational Expenditure (OpEx) |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| One-time investment | Ongoing operational cost |
| Purchase hardware | Pay for services |
| Organization owns infrastructure | Cloud provider owns infrastructure |
| High upfront cost | Pay-as-you-go pricing |
| Typical for on-premises | Typical for cloud computing |

## Microsoft Trigger Words

### CapEx

If a question contains words such as:

- upfront cost
- hardware purchase
- buy servers
- one-time investment
- datacenter

Think:

> Capital Expenditure (CapEx)

### OpEx

If a question contains words such as:

- monthly cost
- subscription
- pay-as-you-go
- ongoing cost
- consumption-based

Think:

> Operational Expenditure (OpEx)

## Common Exam Questions

Microsoft frequently asks questions such as:

- Which expenditure model requires purchasing hardware?
- Which financial model is commonly associated with cloud computing?
- Which pricing model allows organizations to pay only for what they use?

## Common Mistakes

❌ Thinking CapEx is simply "expensive."

CapEx describes **how infrastructure is purchased**, not whether it is expensive.

❌ Thinking OpEx means "monthly subscription."

Although many cloud services are billed monthly, the defining characteristic of OpEx is paying for resources as operational expenses rather than purchasing infrastructure.

## Exam Tip

Ask how the organization pays for the resource.

If the organization:

> Buys and owns infrastructure upfront

→ **CapEx**

If the organization:

> Pays for services as they are consumed

→ **OpEx**

Remember:

**CapEx = ownership and upfront investment**

**OpEx = consumption and ongoing expense**
